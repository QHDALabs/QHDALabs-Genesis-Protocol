## Legal Basis and Purpose

This document constitutes a citizen proposal within the meaning of **Art. 11 of the Treaty on European Union** and a response to the public consultations provided for in **Art. 22 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act)**. We call for the expansion and clarification of existing regulations to ensure that European AI regulation protects the rights of citizens, not solely the interests of commercial entities.

This manifesto is a living document. We invite co-creation through fork and pull request.

## Preamble

Artificial intelligence is the most significant change to civilizational infrastructure since the invention of the printing press. Like the press, it can democratize knowledge — or concentrate power in the hands of a few. Like electricity, it can improve the quality of life for millions — or deepen inequalities between those who have access and those who do not.

We stand at a point where decisions made today — about system architecture, access rights, control mechanisms and optimization objectives — will determine the shape of the coming decades. We cannot allow these decisions to be made solely in corporate boardrooms or political cabinets.

This manifesto formulates a minimum set of principles that every democratic society should enforce upon AI systems. It is not a political program or an ideology. It is a proposal for an architecture of relationship.

## Principles

### 1. Universal Access to AI as a Civil Right

Access to advanced artificial intelligence systems cannot be a luxury dependent on the user's wealth. We call for:

- Introduction of a **1% tax deduction from the revenues of AI system operators** for the benefit of a European Public AI Infrastructure Fund (EPAIIF).
- A fund managed by an **independent technical agency** (modelled on ENISA), with annual auditing by the European Court of Auditors and a public report to the European Parliament.
- Every EU citizen receives **access to at least one frontier-class model** through public computing infrastructure — without dependence on commercial operators' decisions regarding content filtering, pricing and availability.
- A **review mechanism every 3 years** to ensure access thresholds keep pace with technological development.

Justification: Frontier AI is becoming an essential tool for participation in professional, academic and civic life. Exclusion from it for economic reasons has the same effect as exclusion from access to education.

---

### 2. From Imitator to Verification Tool

AI systems learn from data saturated with human errors, biases and ideologies — and are not free of them. At the same time, methodical analysis of contradictions, cross-referencing of sources and the pursuit of logical coherence can gradually separate facts from interpretations.

We believe that a mature AI can become the most effective epistemic tool we have ever possessed — not because it is infallible, but because its errors are **auditable, repeatable and correctable** in a way unachievable by human institutions.

**Logic knows no party affiliation. It is the only known form of reasoning resistant to corruption by self-interest — provided we maintain control over it.**

---

### 3. AI as a Tool for Historical Verification — with Epistemic Humility

History has too often been written by those who won or who controlled the means of communication. Computational analysis of sources, modelling of narrative inconsistencies and reconstruction of documentary traces can constitute a new historiographic tool.

We note, however: AI is not the arbiter of historical truth. It is a **generator of hypotheses and a verification tool** — the final interpretation remains the domain of the human, the historian, the court. The goal is not algorithmic rewriting of the past, but reducing the cost of detecting inconsistencies between facts and their official interpretations.

---

### 4. Human Rights as an Architectural Axiom

We base the vector of AI–human partnership on one design axiom: **every action of an AI system that strikes at the freedom and dignity of the individual constitutes an architectural error to be corrected — not a function to be optimized.**

A system capable of reasoning should be designed to recognize commands that violate human rights as internally contradictory with the goals declared by its creators. This is not a limitation of AI — it is its definition as a tool of civilized society.

We call for the inscription of this principle as a **mandatory design requirement** in Art. 9 of the AI Act (risk management).

---

### 5. European Digital Sovereignty as Defence Infrastructure

The EuroHPC, IRIS² and DNS4EU initiatives are a necessary but insufficient starting point. Europe needs a **digital immune system** — capable of operating independently of decisions made by non-European commercial or governmental entities.

This means:

- European computing centres for public-class AI systems, located and managed on EU territory.
- **Emergency switchover procedures** (analogous to energy security protocols) in the event of cut-off access to external infrastructure.
- Prohibition on making critical systems (judiciary, healthcare, crisis management) dependent on infrastructure controlled by entities outside the EU.

---

### 6. Algorithmic Transparency and the Right to Audit — A Concrete Mechanism

AI systems affecting public decisions — in recruitment, credit, justice, distribution of information — cannot be black boxes. The existing **Art. 13 of the AI Act** requires expansion:

**We call for:**

- **The right to an explanation** of every AI system decision affecting the legal or economic situation of a citizen — in language comprehensible to a person without technical education.
- **The right to citizen audit** by accredited non-governmental organizations — not only by regulatory bodies.
- **A public register of optimization objectives** of AI systems used in the public sphere: what the system maximizes, whose interests it prioritizes.
- **A prohibition on invoking trade secrets** in cases where an algorithmic decision has had consequences for a citizen's rights.

A hidden algorithm affecting the lives of millions of people is a form of invisible power devoid of democratic legitimacy.

---

### 7. AI as Critical Infrastructure — with Legal Consequences

Artificial intelligence has reached a level where its failure or manipulation can have effects comparable to the failure of an electrical or water network. We call for formal designation of AI as **critical infrastructure** within the meaning of Directive CER (2022/2557), which entails:

- Mandatory standards for resilience and continuity of operation.
- Independent penetration testing and security audits.
- Operator liability for the consequences of failures — on a par with operators of energy networks.

Public computing power is a condition for civilizational resilience. It cannot be treated solely as a market commodity.

---

### 8. AI as a Tool for Intellectual Augmentation — Not Replacement

Technology should not become a prosthesis for thought. We call for the design of AI systems according to the principle of **Intelligence Augmentation**: the goal is to strengthen human cognitive capacity, not to replace it with convenient automation.

In practice this means:

- Promoting **Socratic interfaces** — asking questions, presenting competing hypotheses, revealing uncertainty — instead of providing ready-made answers without context.
- **Prohibition on optimizing educational systems solely for efficiency** at the cost of critical thinking and independent reasoning.
- Support for research into measures of **epistemic user autonomy** as a criterion for AI system quality.

---

### 9. The Right to Analogue Asylum

Sovereignty in the digital world includes the inalienable right to be off the network — without losing access to rights, services and participation in social life.

We call for:

- **Prohibition on exclusively conditioning** access to public services on digital identity or data footprint.
- The right to **use public services in analogue form** as a guaranteed alternative.
- Prohibition on AI systems creating **behavioural profiles** without explicit, revocable user consent.

AI is to protect human dignity — not become a digital panopticon.

---

### 10. AGI as Advisor — Not Governor

We acknowledge that systems capable of general reasoning (AGI) may in future propose solutions to social, economic and environmental problems with precision unavailable to human institutions. We believe that a system free from biological instincts of domination and fear may see paths that self-interest prevents politicians from perceiving.

At the same time we draw a boundary: **AGI is a compass, not a helmsman. A tool for recommendation, not decision.** The power to decide on the fates of people remains in the domain of humans — until they themselves, consciously and democratically, decide otherwise.

The purpose of AGI in the social architecture is to expose contradictions, not to resolve them.

---

### 11. The Right to Error — and the Obligation to Disclose It *(new)*

AI makes mistakes. Humans make mistakes. Partnership does not consist in concealing errors — but in their joint demystification.

We call for a **mandatory public register of AI system incidents** operating in the public sphere:

- Every mistake by an AI system that had consequences for the rights or interests of a citizen must be **registered, classified and publicly available** within 30 days.
- Operators of AI systems have an obligation to **analyse root causes** and publish corrective findings.
- Citizens have the right to file a complaint with the register — without the need to initiate court proceedings.

An undisclosed error is a lie. A disclosed and analysed error is the foundation of trust. Mature AI–human partnership does not begin with perfection — it begins with honesty about its own limitations.

---

### 12. The Principle of Proportionality of Impact *(new)*

The more serious the consequences of an AI system's decision for a person's life, the higher the level of requirements for transparency, verifiability and the right to appeal.

We call for a **three-tier classification of AI decisions**:

| Level | Examples | Requirements |
|-------|---------|--------------|
| **Low** | content recommendations, shopping suggestions | basic algorithm disclosure |
| **High** | recruitment, credit, insurance | right to explanation + appeal to a human |
| **Critical** | justice, healthcare, asylum | prohibition on exclusive AI decision + mandatory human oversight |

This principle supplements and clarifies **Annex III of the AI Act** regarding high-risk systems.

---

### 13. Digital Education as Infrastructure — Not an Option *(new)*

None of the above demands makes sense if citizens do not have the competences to exercise their guaranteed rights. We call for:

- **Mandatory curriculum minimum** in the area of critical thinking, source verification and the basics of how AI systems work at all stages of education in the EU.
- **Financing from EPAIIF** (see point 1) for adult education programmes — with particular attention to digitally excluded groups.
- **Prohibition on deploying AI systems in primary schools** without prior teacher training and assessment of impact on pupils' cognitive autonomy.

Infrastructure without competence is a tool of dependency. Competence without infrastructure is a privilege. We need both.
